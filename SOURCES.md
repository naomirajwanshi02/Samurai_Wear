# Sources

The look of each suit follows the two reference images supplied for this project (`assets/refs/`).
The facts shown in the app come from these general references:

| Claim in the app | Source |
|---|---|
| Kabuto bowls (hachi) are made of riveted iron plates; rivet-bowl helmets are hoshi-bachi; the hole at the top is the tehen | Wikipedia, “Kabuto” — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabuto |
| Shikoro (neck guard), fukigaeshi (turned-back flaps), mabizashi (visor), kuwagata (horn-like crest), maedate | Wikipedia, “Kabuto” |
| Menpō / men-yoroi: iron face guards, often with fierce expressions and moustaches; a small hole or tube under the chin lets sweat drain; yodare-kake throat guard | Wikipedia, “Men-yoroi” — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men-yoroi |
| Ō-yoroi: the “great armour” of the Heian and Kamakura periods, worn by mounted archers; large sode; stencilled-leather chest panel (tsurubashiri) | Wikipedia, “Ō-yoroi” — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8C-yoroi |
| Tōsei gusoku: “modern armour” of the Sengoku period onward | Wikipedia, “Tōsei-gusoku” — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dsei-gusoku |
| Odoshi lacing; kebiki (dense) and sugake (spaced) lacing; colour names such as kon-ito and aka-ito | Wikipedia, “Odoshi” — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odoshi |
| Japanese stag beetles are called kuwagata-mushi after the helmet crest | Wikipedia, “Lucanidae” (Japanese name) and “Kabuto” |
| Ō-yoroi weighed about 25–30 kg | Wikipedia, “Ō-yoroi” |
| Yabusame (mounted archery) is still performed today | Wikipedia, “Yabusame” — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yabusame |
| Families display helmets and armour on Children’s Day (5 May) | Wikipedia, “Children's Day (Japan)” — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children%27s_Day_(Japan) |

Before publishing, re-check each claim against the linked page; the weight and the sweat-hole facts
vary between sources and are worded as “about” and “many”.
